## Terms explained here (first time said)
container ("one big steel box on a ship"), shipping line ("the company that runs the ship"), free time, demurrage, detention, customs ("the government's check on imports"), freight ("what you pay to move the box").
TEU and FCL/LCL are **not** mentioned (they belong to the "what a container holds" episode).

## Deferred to part two
- Port / terminal storage charges (ground rent, CFS storage), which the terminal bills separately from the shipping line's demurrage.
- Combined "demurrage + detention" free time offered by some lines; who pays under each Incoterm; disputes and waivers.

## Facts that need a primary source (ask before searching)
- **Who charges what:** demurrage = shipping line's charge for a full container kept inside the port/terminal past free time; detention = shipping line's charge for keeping the container outside the port past free time until returned empty. (Definitions vary by country; in some places "demurrage" covers both.) Source: a carrier's published D&D tariff (e.g. a major line's India import tariff), or the US FMC 2020 interpretive rule on D&D for definitions.
- **Free time "often around five days"** for imports at Indian ports — check carrier tariffs for Nhava Sheva (JNPA), Mumbai.
- **"Around $100 a day" for a 20 ft container**, rising in slabs — check the same tariffs (rates differ by line, box type and day slab).
- **Ten days × ~$100 ≈ $1,000** is illustrative (it simplifies: it ignores the free days and slab increases); keep "≈" on screen or adjust the numbers after checking.
- That port storage is billed **separately** from demurrage at Indian terminals/CFSs (for the part-two teaser).
- The rupee rate on the recording day.
