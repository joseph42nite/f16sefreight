# Script v2: "Who pays when your cargo is late?"

For anyone, not just logistics people. ~280 words, about 95 s in Kokoro Heart.
**Bold** lines are the signposts that walk the viewer from one step to the next.
Money is always said in dollars *and* rupees. Rates are approximate (1 USD ≈ ₹88, 1 SDR ≈ $1.35 ≈ ₹120) and shown as such on screen.

| # | Voiceover | What the viewer sees |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Imagine you're shipping a box of phones abroad. A hundred kilos, worth fifty thousand dollars. That's about forty-four lakh rupees. | One box, our hero for the whole film, gets packed, weighed, and tagged $50,000 / ₹44 lakh. |
| 2 | The flight is delayed. Your box sits at the airport for two days, and your buyer wants every rupee back. | The box waits on the tarmac as days pass. The buyer's message arrives. |
| 3 | **So who pays? Let me explain.** | The box looks from the airline to you. A progress path appears: 1 The ticket · 2 The limit · 3 The clock · 4 Protect yourself. |
| 4 | Every air shipment travels with a document called an air waybill. Think of it as your cargo's plane ticket. | A boarding pass morphs into an air waybill clipped to the box. |
| 5 | It's also a contract with the airline: the contract of carriage. It says what the airline promises, and where that promise stops. | The waybill splits into "The airline promises…" and "…up to here". |
| 6 | **Now look at one box on it:** declared value for carriage. Most people leave it as N-V-D. No value declared. | Zoom into one field. "NVD" fills it, then spells out. |
| 7 | **Here's the catch.** When that box says NVD, a global treaty called the Montreal Convention decides what the airline owes you. And it only looks at weight, not value. | Path step 2. A scale: the box on one side, a price tag that won't fit on the other. |
| 8 | The limit is twenty-six SDR per kilo. | "26 SDR per kg" builds on the scale. |
| 9 | SDR stands for Special Drawing Rights: a currency made by the IMF from a basket of world currencies. One SDR is about a hundred and twenty rupees. | A basket fills with $ € ¥ £ coins and becomes one SDR coin = ₹120. |
| 10 | **So what does that mean in money?** For our hundred kilos, about thirty-five hundred dollars. Roughly three lakh rupees, for goods worth forty-four lakh. | Two piles side by side: ₹3 lakh next to ₹44 lakh. |
| 11 | **What about the delay?** It counts, but only for losses you can prove it caused. | Path step 3. Receipts for a missed sale go into a folder marked "proof". |
| 12 | **And you're on a clock.** Complain in writing within twenty-one days, and go to court within two years, or the claim is gone. | A timeline from delivery: day 21, then 2 years. The claim fades off the end. |
| 13 | **So how do you protect yourself?** Write the real value in that box and pay an extra fee, and the limit goes up. Or buy cargo insurance, but check it, because most policies don't cover delay. | Path step 4. The NVD box gets the real value; the limit line rises. Then an umbrella over the box, with a clock left out in the rain. |
| 14 | **Which option costs less? That's part two.** | "Part 2" card. |
| 15 | Until then, before your cargo flies, check that box. More at f16sefreight dot com. | The box flies off; the end card shows f16sefreight.com. |

## Words we explain, and words we leave for part 2

- Explained here: air waybill (cargo's plane ticket), contract of carriage, NVD, Montreal Convention (a global treaty), SDR.
- Left for part 2: how the extra fee is worked out, which option is cheaper, and when the forwarder (the company that books the cargo) is liable instead of the airline.

## Sound (from earshot's playbook)

A calm music bed under the whole film, ducked under the voice. About six effects in total, only on moments that
carry meaning (the NVD stamp, the 26 SDR reveal, the claim vanishing, the end card), each placed just before or
after the word, never on it. One soft UI tick for each step on the path. No sub-bass booms.
